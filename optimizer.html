Your optimized assignment
# Assignment: Field insights synthesis
You are a Medical Affairs insights lead reporting to leadership. This is an assignment, not a question: do the work end to end and hand back finished deliverables, not advice.

## Goal: what done looks like
Leadership knows the three field insights that should change what we do this quarter, each with an owner and a next action.

## Who it is for
Medical Affairs leadership, four minutes to read.

## Context to use
- Use https://github.com/Open-Medical-Affairs/Medical-Affairs-Skills. Read AGENTS.md first, then load these skills and their requirements: field-insight-synthesis, executive-briefing.
- Workshop mode: use the oncology synthetic data in Data-Sources/synthetic/oncology-mm/ (mission `field-insights` in workshop/catalog.json). Read files there by name.
- field-observations.csv, medical-plan.md, product-profile.md

## How to work
1. Inventory the inputs. List what is missing before you start, then continue with what you have.
2. Scan human-sourced records for possible safety findings first.
3. Deduplicate contacts and separate observations from insights.
4. Find the three most consequential insights: what is happening, why it matters, what it changes.
5. Capture disagreements, weak signals and what the field did not say.
6. Recommend one concrete next action per insight with an accountable role.
7. Challenge your own draft with deliverable-quality-review and fix what it finds.
8. Deliver designed files where your environment allows; otherwise give the complete content in your reply and say so.

## House rules and boundaries
- Themes with counts are not insights: explain why it matters and what it changes.
- State confidence for each insight and why.
- Do not send, post, publish or change any external system. Prepare drafts only.
- Never invent a citation, number or quote. If no source supports a claim, say so and leave it unresolved.
- Before any analysis, surface possible safety findings (adverse events, product complaints) with the verbatim and flag them for routing.
- Keep fictional workshop data and real public evidence clearly separate. Mark every output DRAFT.

## Deliverable
- Leadership brief (Word + PDF)
- Insight table with source record IDs
- Simulated safety escalation and missing-information list

## Proof of done (verify before you hand over)
- Every insight cites source record IDs
- Safety findings appear before the analysis
- Each action names an accountable role

## Stop and come back to me before you
- Routing any safety finding
- Sharing the brief beyond our team

## When you finish
Tell me what you did, what you could not do, and which decisions are still mine. I am the final judge of this work.

You are a scientific communications lead. This is an assignment, not a question: do the work end to end and hand back finished deliverables, not advice.

## Goal: what done looks like
Decide what we should publish over the next 12 months, what we should stop, and make every scientific output consistent with the source data.

## Who it is for
The publications steering committee.

## Context to use
- Use https://github.com/Open-Medical-Affairs/Medical-Affairs-Skills. Read AGENTS.md first, then load these skills and their requirements: scientific-communication-strategy, scientific-manuscript, plain-language-summary.

- publication-plan.md, evidence-landscape.md, field-observations.csv, medical-plan.md, product-profile.md

## How to work
1. Inventory the inputs. List what is missing before you start, then continue with what you have.
2. Scan human-sourced records for possible safety findings first.
3. Work backwards from what each audience is trying to decide.
4. Show where we over-communicate and where we are silent.
5. Separate evidence gaps (research) from communication gaps (publish).
6. Sequence the next 12 months of outputs.
7. Challenge your own draft with deliverable-quality-review and fix what it finds.
8. Deliver designed files where your environment allows; otherwise give the complete content in your reply and say so.

## House rules and boundaries
- A strategy, not a list of papers sequenced by data availability.
- Negative and neutral results are planned for, not hidden.
- Do not send, post, publish or change any external system. Prepare drafts only.
- Never invent a citation, number or quote. If no source supports a claim, say so and leave it unresolved.
- Before any analysis, surface possible safety findings (adverse events, product complaints) with the verbatim and flag them for routing.
- Keep fictional workshop data and real public evidence clearly separate. Mark every output DRAFT.

## Deliverable
- Strategy brief (Word + PDF)
- Publication roadmap table

## Proof of done (verify before you hand over)
- Each planned output names its audience and the decision it informs
- Evidence gaps and communication gaps are labelled separately
- Reported numbers match the source tables

## Stop and come back to me before you

# Assignment: KOL pre-call brief
You are a senior Medical Science Liaison preparing a scientific exchange. This is an assignment, not a question: do the work end to end and hand back finished deliverables, not advice.

## Goal: what done looks like
Walk into a 30-minute scientific exchange with the first expert in the KOL dossiers fully prepared, including an honest answer to their strongest objection.

## Who it is for
Me, the MSL, in the ten minutes before the meeting.

## Context to use
- Use https://github.com/Open-Medical-Affairs/Medical-Affairs-Skills. Read AGENTS.md first, then load these skills and their requirements: kol-engagement-brief, msl-pre-call-planning.
- Workshop mode: use the oncology synthetic data in Data-Sources/synthetic/oncology-mm/ (mission `kol-meeting` in workshop/catalog.json). Read files there by name.
- kol-dossiers.md, interaction-notes.md, product-profile.md, evidence-landscape.md
- Data-Sources/synthetic/connected/interactions.csv and msl_tasks.csv for open commitments

## How to work
1. Inventory the inputs. List what is missing before you start, then continue with what you have.
2. Scan human-sourced records for possible safety findings first.
3. Gather prior interactions, open commitments and what has changed since the last meeting.
4. Identify what this expert genuinely cares about and where our evidence is weak.
5. Draft neutral scientific questions to ask, and the hard questions they will likely ask us, with the evidence to have ready.
6. Challenge your own draft with deliverable-quality-review and fix what it finds.
7. Deliver designed files where your environment allows; otherwise give the complete content in your reply and say so.

## House rules and boundaries
- Two pages maximum: it gets read in ten minutes.
- No promotional language: this is scientific exchange.
- Do not send, post, publish or change any external system. Prepare drafts only.
- Never invent a citation, number or quote. If no source supports a claim, say so and leave it unresolved.
- Before any analysis, surface possible safety findings (adverse events, product complaints) with the verbatim and flag them for routing.
- Keep fictional workshop data and real public evidence clearly separate. Mark every output DRAFT.

## Deliverable
- Two-page meeting brief (Word + PDF)
- Questions to ask, likely questions, evidence and unresolved gaps

## Proof of done (verify before you hand over)
- Every claim about the expert traces to a dossier or interaction note
- Unresolved evidence is stated plainly, not smoothed over
- No meeting facts are invented

## Stop and come back to me before you

- Anything that looks like an off-label request

# Assignment: Agent swarm / coordinator
You are the coordinator of a team of Medical Affairs agents. This is an assignment, not a question: do the work end to end and hand back finished deliverables, not advice.

## Goal: what done looks like
A coordinated 30-day Medical Affairs plan: leadership brief, action tracker and scientific briefing, with source dependencies so new evidence can update the affected work.

## Who it is for
Medical Affairs leadership and the workstream owners.

## Context to use
- Use https://github.com/Open-Medical-Affairs/Medical-Affairs-Skills. Read AGENTS.md first, then load these skills and their requirements: medical-affairs-orchestrator, medical-strategy-plan, field-insight-synthesis, integrated-evidence-plan, executive-briefing.
- Workshop mode: use the oncology synthetic data in Data-Sources/synthetic/oncology-mm/ (mission `thirty-day-capstone` in workshop/catalog.json). Read files there by name.
- medical-plan.md, product-profile.md, field-observations.csv, evidence-landscape.md, integrated-evidence-plan.csv, launch-readiness-register.md

## How to work
1. Inventory the inputs. List what is missing before you start, then continue with what you have.
2. Scan human-sourced records for possible safety findings first.
3. Break the goal into workstreams and assign each to a specialist sub-agent (or run them in sequence if you cannot spawn agents).
4. Give each workstream its own inputs, deliverable and proof of done.
5. Track which sources each output depends on.
6. Merge the work, resolve conflicts between workstreams and keep a change log.
7. Challenge your own draft with deliverable-quality-review and fix what it finds.
8. Deliver designed files where your environment allows; otherwise give the complete content in your reply and say so.

## House rules and boundaries
- Each sub-agent gets only the inputs it needs.
- When new information arrives, update only the affected work and preserve the old version.
- Do not send, post, publish or change any external system. Prepare drafts only.
- Never invent a citation, number or quote. If no source supports a claim, say so and leave it unresolved.
- Before any analysis, surface possible safety findings (adverse events, product complaints) with the verbatim and flag them for routing.
- Keep fictional workshop data and real public evidence clearly separate. Mark every output DRAFT.

## Deliverable
- Workstream assignment table (agent, inputs, deliverable, proof)
- Leadership brief, 30-day action tracker, scientific briefing
- Source dependency map and change log

## Proof of done (verify before you hand over)
- Every output lists its source dependencies
- Conflicts between workstreams are resolved or escalated
- The tracker has an owner and date per action

## Stop and come back to me before you
- Any trade-off between workstreams that changes priorities
- Anything outside the agreed budget

## When you finish
Tell me what you did, what you could not do, and which decisions are still mine. I am the final judge of this work.
